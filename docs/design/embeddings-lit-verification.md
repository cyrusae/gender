## Corrections that change the study plan

1. **Wrong arXiv ID in B (2210.13451).** Use 2206.01691. Check and fix the Zotero record VM62ICFA before the bibliography agent runs, or it will import a physics paper.
2. **Reproducing Omrani Table 5 needs 28 iterations, not 15.** The released `Results/es.xlsx` (iter 0–28) reproduces the paper's Tables 2 and 5 only at iteration 28 (GG 1.235; GenS 0.036; GenC 1.523). At iteration 15 GG-WEAT is 1.305 and GenS −0.006. The paper's text and Figs 3/5 say 15. Plan: run the erasure to chance *and* to 28 and report both; record the discrepancy in `decisions.md`. Also note the probe reaches chance (~0.51) by iteration 15, yet GG-WEAT keeps falling slowly to 28, i.e. the erased subspace is not what drives all of the remaining GG-WEAT change. This is evidence that the "resistance" is partly the measure, not the erasure.
3. **The fastText subword speculation is narrower than A says.** Omrani use it for the Word2Vec contrast (fewer iterations needed), not as an explanation for ES/PL resistance. A study that claims "Omrani suggested the ending / subword cause" would misattribute. The ending-matched vs regular noun control (C1/C2) remains novel: nobody in the papers read controls for spelling.
4. **Single-category GG-WEAT differs from Zhou's b_w in sign, scale and attributes.** If C1 claims to run "Zhou's inanimate metric", use |s| with Zhou's attribute definition, and label the Omrani-style signed effect size separately.
5. **Fewer items than assumed in Omrani.** 10,000 inanimate nouns total means 2,000 per language (about 1,000 per gender), not 4,000.
6. **The substrate is not a differentiator.** Both Zhou and Omrani use fastText. B's "static fastText vs GloVe-style" differentiator is false; what is novel is the control (endings/regular vs exception), the use of Wiktionary gold labels, and the English-attribute arm in aligned MUSE space (Omrani's English is used only as a pairwise-distance reference).
7. **Gonen 2019 is not Spanish.** Do not cite it for ES; it supports DE/IT only. Gonen's contextual-neutralization result (remove agreement cues in training) is the closest prior evidence that the grammatical signal is carried by agreement context, which is relevant to C2's "what erasure costs".
8. **Xiao & Li cover ES and FR contextual models and already compute a per-noun WEAT-style inanimate score.** The planned "parked transformer study" should cite it as prior art for the inanimate-score idea; their data uses LLM-generated noun lists (not Wiktionary), so nothing to reuse under the gold-label rule. They do not cite Omrani & Caliskan 2022.
9. **Zhou did have a grammatical test (word-pair translation by analogy)** on the bilingual embeddings. C1's claim "Zhou checked nothing grammatical" should be phrased "Zhou did not check whether grammatical gender of inanimate nouns survives the mitigation".
