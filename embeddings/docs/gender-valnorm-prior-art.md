# Prior art: "gender ValNorm" (SC-WEAT gender score vs. human word-gender ratings)

Searched 2026-11-08 (web search + full-text PDFs read with pdftotext). "READ" = I opened the paper's text
and the claim comes from it. "SNIPPET" = only seen in a search-result summary (treat as UNVERIFIED).

## 1. Verdict: PARTLY DONE (English only; the grammatical-gender / inanimate-noun version not found)

- The core move (embedding gender score for each word, correlated with human Glasgow Norms gender ratings)
  **has been done in English**: Lewis & Lupyan 2020 (Study 1a) and Valentini et al. 2023 (ACL short).
  Neither calls it ValNorm, neither uses the SC-WEAT effect size (they use a plain difference of mean
  cosines to 8 female vs 8 male anchors), and neither frames it as an intrinsic evaluation of the embedding.
- The ValNorm framing (single-category association score, correlated with human norms, used to check
  embedding quality across languages) exists only for **valence**. In ValNorm itself, gender is used only
  as the contrast case ("social biases vary across languages"), not as a norm-correlation task.
- **Not found** (searched; absence is not proof): the same correlation for Spanish/French/German (or any
  grammatical-gender language) against human gender ratings of words; any restriction to inanimate nouns;
  any before/after-debiasing comparison against human gender-rating norms; any use on LLM/contextual
  representations. Human word-level gender norms exist outside English only for Dutch (Vankrunkelsven et al.,
  READ abstract) and for *role nouns* (Misersky et al. 2014; SNIPPET), not for general/inanimate nouns in ES/FR/DE
  as far as I found. Spanish/French/German equivalents of Glasgow GEND: not found.

## 3. What would be new in our version

1. **SC-WEAT/ValNorm framing for gender** (effect size per word, bootstrap/permutation, reported as an intrinsic quality check), rather than raw mean-cosine differences.
2. **Grammatical-gender languages**: ES/FR/DE embeddings (and LLM representations) against a human word-gender norm. Only English (Glasgow) and Dutch (non-embedding) norms were found; so this needs a new or translated norm, or an English-norm via translation pairs (note CLAUDE.md: PI cannot check German; ES/FR need automatic filtering).
3. **Inanimate nouns specifically**: test whether the gender score tracks *grammatical* gender versus *human semantic* gender on inanimates (Omrani Sabbaghi & Caliskan show the grammatical signal dominates inanimates but never compare to human ratings; Vankrunkelsven says semantic gender is not grammatical gender). A prediction: for inanimates, the SC-WEAT score should correlate with grammatical gender but weakly with human semantic gender in the same language.
4. **Before/after mitigation** (Zhou De-Align / Hybrid, per the existing C1 analysis): how much of the human-norm correlation survives, split by animate vs inanimate. Nobody was found doing this against human norms.
5. **Cross-lingual comparison of the correlation itself** (as ValNorm did for valence: stable across languages) with the hypothesis in reverse: gender-norm correlation should *vary* with grammatical-gender system.

## 4. English validation numbers to expect

- Lewis & Lupyan (fastText, 8+8 anchors, mean-cosine difference, 4,671 Glasgow words): **r = 0.63 (subtitles), 0.59 (Wikipedia)**.
- Valentini et al. (GloVe/SGNS, Glasgow, 4.6k words): **r = 0.44-0.58 unweighted** (GloVe 0.49/0.44, SGNS 0.55/0.50, subtitles/Wikipedia); weighted up to 0.72.
- So an English SC-WEAT-vs-Glasgow replication should land around **0.45-0.65**, clearly lower than valence ValNorm (0.82-0.88) and lower than WEFAT vs occupation % women (0.90) and androgynous names (0.84). A result near 0.9 would be suspicious; near 0.6 is a match.
- Caveats for matching: Glasgow is rated on 1 (feminine) - 7 (masculine), so sign-flip; Lewis & Lupyan and Valentini average homonyms; both use subtitle-trained embeddings as best case; SC-WEAT effect size divides by the std of the word's similarities to all attribute words, which may change ranks versus mean-cosine difference (not tested by anyone I found).
- Spanish/French/German: no published number found to compare to.

*Spot-check (Claude, 2026-11-08): Valentini et al. 2023 Table 1 confirmed from the PDF text: Glasgow-Gender r = 0.58/0.49/0.55 (PMI/GloVe/SGNS, OpenSubtitles), 0.50/0.44/0.50 (Wikipedia); attribute words female, woman, girl, sister, she, her, hers, daughter vs male counterparts; Glasgow homonyms averaged, scale flipped (8 − rating). Same authors also wrote "The undesirable dependence on frequency of gender bias metrics" (2022), relevant to our German frequency-axis finding.*
